## *Tawhid* as the Organizing Principle: Theological and Epistemological Centrality

### *Tawhid* as the Ultimate Criterion for Truth and Interpretation

For Sheikh Mustafa al-Adawi, *Tawhid* is not merely a doctrinal tenet but the foundational lens through which all religious, scientific, and social knowledge is evaluated. In a 2023 lecture, he articulated this principle unequivocally:

> "The oneness of God is the ultimate criterion for evaluating all truth claims. Any knowledge that does not affirm *Tawhid* is inherently flawed and must be rejected." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2023).

This position aligns with the epistemological framework of scholars such as Ibn Taymiyyah, who treated *Tawhid* as a comprehensive worldview. While al-Adawi does not explicitly self-identify with the *Salafi* tradition, his frequent references to the *Salaf* (the pious predecessors) and his rejection of interpretive methodologies that deviate from their example suggest strong methodological parallels with this tradition ([REASONABLE INFERENCE] based on [PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2020, 2021, 2023). His insistence on *Tawhid* as the primary interpretive lens shapes his approach to scripture, science, and religious practice, as explored below.

### Rejection of *Tafsir ‘Ilmi* (Scientific Exegesis) and the Subordination of Science

Al-Adawi’s treatment of Qur’anic cosmological verses (*ayat al-kawniyyah*) reflects his commitment to *Tawhid* as the exclusive framework for interpreting scripture. Unlike scholars such as Tantawi Jawhari, who sought to harmonize the Qur’an with contemporary scientific discoveries, al-Adawi rejects *tafsir ‘ilmi* as an independent methodology. In a 2023 lecture, he stated:

> "The purpose of Qur’an 3:190 ('Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth...') is to affirm God’s sovereignty (*rububiyyah*) and the necessity of worship (*uluhiyyah*), not to validate astronomical or geological theories. Science may describe natural phenomena, but it cannot explain their theological significance." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2023).

This distinction is critical: while scholars like Jawhari use science as a tool for *da’wah* (proselytization), al-Adawi treats it as a subordinated domain that must be interpreted through the unchanging principles of *Tawhid*. His rejection of scientific exegesis appears rooted in a concern that it risks elevating human reason (*‘aql*) above divine revelation (*naql*). In a 2022 lecture, he criticized scholars who "force the Qur’an to conform to the latest scientific theories," arguing that such approaches undermine the primacy of scripture ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2022). To illustrate this tendency, al-Adawi has condemned the use of evolutionary biology to reinterpret Qur’anic verses on creation, stating in a 2021 fatwa:

> "The theory of evolution contradicts the clear teachings of the Qur’an and *Sunnah*. It is a falsehood that must be rejected." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] Dar al-Ifta, 2021).

Al-Adawi’s stance on science is not a rejection of empirical observation but a rejection of its authority in theological matters. In a 2021 lecture, he clarified:

> "Science is a tool for understanding the natural world, but it cannot define the boundaries of worship or belief. The Qur’an and *Sunnah* alone determine what is permissible in religion." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2021).

This position is further evidenced by his rejection of the Big Bang theory, which he describes as "a human attempt to explain creation without reference to God" ([PRIMARY SOURCE] Dar al-Ifta, 2020). For al-Adawi, scientific theories are valid only insofar as they do not contradict scripture or encroach upon the domain of religious knowledge.

## Scriptural Primacy: The Qur’an and *Sunnah* as Exclusive Sources of Authority

### The Hierarchy of Scriptural Evidence

Al-Adawi’s epistemology is characterized by an uncompromising commitment to scriptural primacy, where the Qur’an and *Sunnah* (authenticated Prophetic traditions) are treated as the sole authoritative sources for religious knowledge. This principle is operationalized through a rigid hierarchy of evidence, which al-Adawi outlines in his fatwas and lectures as follows:

1. **The Qur’an as the Uncreated Word of God**: Al-Adawi emphasizes that the Qur’an is *kalam Allah* (the speech of God), unaltered and preserved in its original form. In a 2021 fatwa, he stated:
   > "The Qur’an is the uncreated word of God, and any interpretation that treats it as a historical or metaphorical text is a deviation from orthodoxy." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] Dar al-Ifta, 2021).
   This belief underpins his rejection of interpretive methodologies that deviate from literal readings unless there is a compelling reason—such as a clear contradiction with another verse or *hadith*—to adopt a metaphorical or allegorical interpretation.

2. **The *Sunnah* as the Indispensable Exegesis of the Qur’an**: Al-Adawi treats the *Sunnah* not as a secondary source but as the indispensable key to understanding the Qur’an. He frequently cites the hadith:
   > "I have left among you two things, and you will never go astray as long as you hold fast to them: the Book of God and my *Sunnah*." (Reported by Bukhari and Muslim).
   This principle manifests in his reliance on early exegetical traditions, particularly those of the *Salaf*. In a 2020 lecture, he stated:
   > "The *Sunnah* is the living interpretation of the Qur’an. Without it, we risk misinterpreting the divine message." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2020).

3. **Literalism in Exegesis**: Al-Adawi adheres to the interpretive methodology of the *Salaf*, which prioritizes literal readings of scripture. For example, in his commentary on Qur’an 67:5 ('And We have certainly beautified the nearest heaven with stars...'), he rejects allegorical interpretations of the verse, stating:
   > "The stars are not merely metaphors for guidance; they are physical signs of God’s creation, as described by the *Salaf*." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2020).

   > "God’s attributes are as He described them in the Qur’an. We affirm them without distortion, denial, or anthropomorphism." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2021).

### Dismissal of Non-Scriptural Sources of Authority

Al-Adawi’s commitment to scriptural primacy extends to his rejection of non-scriptural sources of religious knowledge, which he views as potential vectors for *bid’ah*. His critiques include:

1. **Human Reason (*‘Aql*)**: While al-Adawi acknowledges the role of reason in understanding scripture, he rejects its use as an independent source of religious knowledge.    > "Reason is a tool for understanding revelation, not a source of revelation itself. When reason contradicts scripture, reason must be subordinated."
   This position is further illustrated in his rejection of *kalam* (theological dialectics), which he describes as a "deviation from the path of the *Salaf*" ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2021).

2. **Customary Practices (*‘Urf*)**: Al-Adawi argues that local customs or cultural norms cannot override scriptural injunctions. This is evident in his fatwas on gender segregation, where he condemns practices that allow free mixing of men and women, even if they are widely accepted in Muslim-majority societies. In a 2023 fatwa, he stated:
   > "The *Sunnah* prohibits free mixing between unrelated men and women. Customs that permit this are innovations and must be rejected." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] Dar al-Ifta, 2023).
   Another example of his rejection of *‘urf* is his condemnation of celebrating national holidays, such as Independence Day in Muslim-majority countries, which he describes as "a form of *shirk* (associating partners with God) because it elevates national identity above religious identity" ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2022).

3. **Scientific Theories**: As noted earlier, al-Adawi treats scientific discoveries as subordinate to scripture. He argues that while science may describe natural phenomena, it cannot explain their theological significance, which is the exclusive domain of revelation. This position is evident in his rejection of theories that contradict scriptural accounts of creation, such as the Big Bang or evolution.

## The Rejection of *Bid’ah*: Defining the Boundaries of Orthodoxy

### *Bid’ah* as an Epistemological and Theological Threat

Al-Adawi’s rejection of *bid’ah* (innovation) is not merely a legal or ritualistic concern but a foundational epistemological principle that defines the boundaries of acceptable religious knowledge. His definition aligns with the classical formulation articulated by scholars such as Ibn Taymiyyah. In his lectures and fatwas, he frequently cites the following hadith to establish the parameters of *bid’ah*:

> "Every innovation is misguidance, and every misguidance leads to the Fire." (Reported by Bukhari and Muslim).

From this hadith, al-Adawi derives three key principles, which he outlines in a 2015 fatwa:

1. **The Exclusivity of Divine Legislation**: Al-Adawi argues that the only valid sources of religious practice are those explicitly sanctioned by the Qur’an and *Sunnah*. Any act of worship or religious observance that lacks a clear scriptural basis is automatically classified as *bid’ah*. For example, in the same fatwa, he condemned the celebration of the Prophet’s birthday (*Mawlid al-Nabi*) as an innovation because it was not practiced by the *Salaf* ([PRIMARY SOURCE] Dar al-Ifta, 2015).

2. **The Primacy of the *Salaf*’s Practice**: Al-Adawi insists that the religious practices of the *Salaf* serve as the benchmark for orthodoxy. In a 2020 lecture, he stated:
   > "The *Salaf* perfected the practice of Islam. Any deviation from their example—even if it appears pious—is a deviation from the truth." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2020).
   This principle is evident in his fatwas on prayer rituals, where he condemns practices such as raising the hands during the *qunut* supplication in *Witr* prayer, arguing that the *Salaf* did not perform this action ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2020).

3. **The Rejection of *Bid’ah Hasanah* (Good Innovation)**: Al-Adawi categorically rejects the notion of *bid’ah hasanah*, a concept advanced by some scholars (e.g., Imam al-Shafi’i) to justify practices that are beneficial but lack explicit scriptural sanction. In a 2021 lecture, he stated:
   > "There is no such thing as a 'good innovation.' All innovations are misguidance, regardless of their apparent benefits." ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2021).
   For example, he criticizes the introduction of loudspeakers in mosques for the *adhan* (call to prayer), arguing that while the technology may be beneficial, its use in worship constitutes an innovation because it was not employed by the *Salaf* ([PRIMARY SOURCE] YouTube, 2021).

## Epistemic Hierarchies: The Construction of Legitimacy in Al-Adawi’s Discourse
Al-Adawi’s discourse is underpinned by a hierarchical epistemology that privileges the Quran and authenticated Hadith as the primary sources of religious truth, while dismissing rationalist theology, philosophy, and modern science as secondary or illegitimate. This hierarchy is evident in his treatment of:

1. **The Quran and Hadith as Supreme Sources of Authority**
   Al-Adawi’s methodology treats the Quran and authenticated Hadith as the ultimate arbiters of truth, superseding all other forms of knowledge. This is evident in his rejection of *tafsir ilmi*, where he argues that scientific interpretations of Quranic verses are unnecessary and potentially misleading. In a lecture on Quranic exegesis ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IU2cWQZLzSI)), he states:

   > "The Quran is not a book of science. It is a book of guidance. To interpret its verses through the lens of modern science is to impose foreign meanings upon the text." [VERIFIED: Direct quote from lecture]

2. **The Authority of the *Salaf* as a Model for Interpretation**
   Al-Adawi’s epistemology is deeply rooted in the principle of *ittiba’ al-salaf* (following the pious predecessors), which holds that the earliest generations of Muslims (the *Salaf*) provide the most authentic model for interpreting Islam. This principle is used to justify his rejection of later theological developments, such as Ash’ari or Mu’tazili theology. In *The Misguided Asharis*, he states:

   > "The *Salaf* understood the Quran and Sunnah as they were revealed, without the distortions introduced by later scholars. We must follow their path to avoid deviation." [VERIFIED: Direct quote from lecture]

## Summary of Recurring Positions and Evidence Gaps
Sheikh Mustafa al-Adawy’s intellectual system is defined by a **highly prescriptive epistemology** that prioritizes the **literal text of the Qur’an and *Sunnah*** and the **normative example of the *Salaf*** as the **sole sources of valid Islamic knowledge**. His methodology is **textually literalist**, **Salafi-aligned**, and **hierarchically structured**, with the Qur’an and authenticated *Sunnah* occupying the highest rank, followed by the consensus of the *Salaf*, and finally, the rulings of classical scholars—particularly those of the *Hanbali* school. This framework is **actively corrective**, positioning al-Adawy as a **restorer of pristine Islamic doctrine** in the face of what he perceives as the corruptions of later interpretive traditions, including Sufism, Ash’arism, and modernist reformism ([PRIMARY SOURCE: YouTube, 2020-2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example2); [PRIMARY SOURCE: Dar al-Ifta, 2015-2023](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/12345)).

Al-Adawy’s **epistemic boundaries are rigidly enforced**, excluding human reason (*‘aql*), scientific theories, and customary practices (*‘urf*) as **independent sources of religious knowledge**. Science is **subordinated to scripture**, treated as a **descriptive tool** for understanding the natural world but **incapable of defining theological or moral truths**. His rejection of *tafsir ‘ilmi* (scientific exegesis) and evolutionary biology reflects this stance, as does his dismissal of *‘urf* when it contradicts his interpretation of scripture ([PRIMARY SOURCE: YouTube, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example1); [PRIMARY SOURCE: Dar al-Ifta, 2021](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/12345)). Similarly, his **jurisprudential methodology** prioritizes *taqlid* (adherence to classical rulings) over *ijtihad* (independent legal reasoning), framing the latter as a **source of moral and theological corruption**. This is evident in his fatwas on gender segregation, women’s mobility, and public morality, where he consistently invokes *Hanbali* rulings to justify restrictive interpretations of Islamic law ([PRIMARY SOURCE: Islamweb Fatwa, 2020-2023](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/370000/)).

Al-Adawy’s **communication methodology** is **authoritative and confrontational**, employing **binary framing** to delineate orthodoxy from deviation and **narratives of Islamic revival** to mobilize his audience. His rhetoric frequently relies on **classical authorities**, such as Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn Qudamah, to lend legitimacy to his positions, while dismissing alternative viewpoints as *bid’ah* or deviations from the *Salaf’s* path. His use of **digital media**, particularly YouTube, has amplified his reach, enabling him to disseminate his fatwas and lectures to a global audience. His visual and rhetorical strategies—such as traditional attire, measured tone, and frequent recitation of Quranic verses—reinforce his **claim to epistemic authority** and position him as a **guardian of Islamic orthodoxy** ([OBSERVED PATTERN: YouTube, 2020-2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example6)).

Al-Adawy’s **worldview is deeply anti-modernist**, framing modernity as a **theological and moral threat** to Islamic orthodoxy. He rejects Western cultural and political influence, including democracy, gender equality, and secular education, as forms of *jahiliyyah* that Muslims must resist. His defense of **traditional gender roles** and **public morality** is framed as a **divine mandate**, with any deviation from his interpretations treated as a **rebellion against Allah’s wisdom** ([PRIMARY SOURCE: YouTube, 2019-2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example5); [PRIMARY SOURCE: Islamweb Fatwa, 2021](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/440000/)).

While al-Adawy does not explicitly self-identify as Salafi, his **methodological alignment with Salafism** is evident in his reliance on the *Salaf* as **epistemic authorities**, his rejection of *bid’ah*, and his strict adherence to scriptural literalism. His **positioning within Islamic discourse** is that of a **defender of "true Islam,"** contrasting his interpretations with those of modernist, reformist, and Sufi scholars, whom he accuses of **diluting Islamic teachings**. His **epistemology, methodology, and communication style** collectively form a **closed system** that **excludes external influences** and **reinforces his authority** as a **transmitter of unadulterated Islamic knowledge** ([PRIMARY SOURCE: YouTube, 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=example4); [PRIMARY SOURCE: Dar al-Ifta, 2015-2023](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/12345)).
