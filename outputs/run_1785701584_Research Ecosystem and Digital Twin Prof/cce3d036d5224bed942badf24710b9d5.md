Sheikh Mostafa Al-Adawy represents a significant case study within this phenomenon—a hadith specialist whose digital presence spans over fourteen years and encompasses multiple platforms, reaching audiences across continents with content that addresses both theological foundations and practical questions of Islamic jurisprudence.

This research report presents a comprehensive Digital Twin analysis of the audience ecosystem surrounding Sheikh Al-Adawy, treating the scholar not as the primary subject of biographical inquiry but as an influence source whose digital presence generates measurable effects on audience behavior, community formation, and information diffusion patterns. The analytical framework prioritizes the audience as the primary research target, examining how followers receive, interpret, and act upon content distributed through this multi-platform ecosystem. This approach aligns with broader scholarly efforts to understand how digital religious authority operates in networked publics where traditional hierarchies of scholarly transmission intersect with the democratized access patterns of social media platforms.

The theoretical foundation for this analysis draws upon communication research examining platform affordances and their effects on content distribution, social psychology literature on trust formation and authority perception in digital contexts, and diaspora studies frameworks for understanding how religious content serves identity formation functions for Muslim populations living outside traditional Islamic heartlands.
