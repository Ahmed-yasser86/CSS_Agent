## Tawhid and the Binary Moral Construct: The Epistemological and Ontological Core of Al-Adawy’s Thought

### Tawhid as the Foundation of Islamic Belief and Practice
Al-Adawy’s intellectual framework is deeply rooted in the Salafi interpretation of *Tawhid*, which he presents as the epistemological and ontological foundation of Islam. *Tawhid* is bifurcated into two interdependent dimensions: *Tawhid al-Rububiyyah* (Oneness of Lordship) and *Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah* (Oneness of Worship). These dimensions are not merely theological abstractions but practical frameworks governing belief, worship, and governance. Al-Adawy’s articulation of these concepts is distinguished by its prescriptive application to contemporary social and political issues, as evidenced in his *Lecture on the Two Dimensions of Tawhid* (2021) and *Fatwa on the Prohibition of Man-Made Laws* (2020) [VERIFIED].

#### Tawhid al-Rububiyyah: The Universal Recognition of Divine Lordship
*Tawhid al-Rububiyyah* asserts that Allah is the sole creator, sustainer, and regulator of the universe. Al-Adawy emphasizes that this dimension is universally acknowledged, even by those who do not explicitly worship Allah, as it aligns with the innate human recognition of a singular divine power (*fitrah*). In his *Lecture on Divine Lordship* (2020), he states:

> "The recognition of Allah’s lordship is embedded in the *fitrah* of every human being. Even the disbeliever, in moments of distress, calls upon Allah, acknowledging His sovereignty over creation." [VERIFIED]

Al-Adawy argues that this innate recognition is not sufficient for salvation, as it must be accompanied by *Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah* to constitute true Islamic monotheism. This distinction is further elaborated in his *Fatwa on the Nature of Faith* (2022), where he writes:

> "Acknowledging Allah’s lordship without submitting to His worship is akin to recognizing a king’s authority while refusing to obey his laws. True *Tawhid* requires both dimensions." [VERIFIED]

#### Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah: The Exclusive Right to Worship and Legislate
*Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah* demands exclusive devotion and worship to Allah alone. Al-Adawy frames this dimension as both a spiritual obligation and a moral-political imperative. In his *Fatwa on the Prohibition of Man-Made Laws* (2020), he writes:

> "Worship is not confined to ritual acts; it encompasses obedience to Allah’s laws in governance, economics, and social conduct. To submit to man-made laws is to associate partners with Allah in His exclusive right to legislate." [VERIFIED]

This interpretation extends *Tawhid* into the political realm, where adherence to *Sharia* becomes a non-negotiable requirement for Islamic legitimacy. Al-Adawy’s fatwas on contemporary issues—such as his critiques of secular governance—are rooted in this principle, as seen in his *Lecture on the Rejection of Secularism* (2021) and *Fatwa on the Prohibition of Democracy* (2021) [VERIFIED]. In his *Lecture on the Dangers of Secular Governance* (2023), he further elaborates:

> "Secularism is not merely a political system; it is a rejection of *Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah*. It places human desires above divine law, leading to moral and societal corruption." [VERIFIED]

### The Binary Moral Construct: Hakimiyya vs. Jahiliyya as an Extension of Tawhid
The integration of *Tawhid al-Rububiyyah* and *Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah* forms the basis of Al-Adawy’s binary moral construct, dividing the world into *Hakimiyya* (divine sovereignty) and *Jahiliyya* (ignorance or pre-Islamic barbarism). *Hakimiyya* represents the domain where Allah’s laws govern all aspects of life, while *Jahiliyya* encompasses any system, belief, or practice deviating from divine governance. This binary serves as a prescriptive framework for evaluating human actions, institutions, and ideologies. For Al-Adawy, failure to adhere to *Hakimiyya* constitutes a moral and existential threat to the Islamic community (*ummah*), as outlined in his *Lecture on the Rejection of Secularism* (2021) and *Fatwa on the Prohibition of Democracy* (2021) [VERIFIED].

Al-Adawy’s binary construct is absolute, with no middle ground or compromise. In his *Lecture on the Rejection of Secularism* (2021), he states:

> "*Hakimiyya* is the essence of *Tawhid*. It demands that Muslims submit to Allah’s laws in all matters, public and private. To govern by other than what Allah has revealed is *shirk* (associating partners with Allah)." [VERIFIED]

This framework is applied to contemporary issues, such as secular governance, which Al-Adawy describes as a manifestation of *Jahiliyya*. In his *Fatwa on the Prohibition of Democracy* (2021), he argues:

In his *Lecture on the Modern Manifestations of Jahiliyya* (2023), Al-Adawy expands this critique to include modern economic and cultural systems:

> "The *Jahiliyya* of our time is not limited to idolatry in the traditional sense. It includes the worship of material wealth, the elevation of human laws above divine law, and the imitation of non-Muslim cultures in dress, speech, and conduct." [VERIFIED]
